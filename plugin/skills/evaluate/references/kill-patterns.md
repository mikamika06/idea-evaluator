## 1. How the verdict uses this file
There are exactly three verdicts: `DEAD`, `ALIVE`, `INSUFFICIENT_DATA`. The conductor fills a pattern sheet (section 8) from the answer cards, and `bin/decide` turns the sheet into the verdict:

Only counted patterns can kill (section 2, "Counted toward DEAD"): a fired pattern that rests on the card, on an assumption, on a claim the audit marks `weak`, or on a KP9 ceiling inside the margin band still fires (it blocks ALIVE) but goes to `to_collect` instead of toward DEAD.
- `DEAD` rule D1: one counted strong pattern (KP1-KP7, KP9, KP13-KP16) meets its threshold and no allowed rebuttal (section 6) is present.
- `DEAD` rule D2: three or more counted patterns (strong ones below threshold or rebutted, and supporting ones) rest on independent evidence: each has its own verified finding from its own site, no finding or site serves two of them, and KP4 with KP6 counts once; at least one of them is a core pattern (KP1, KP2, KP4, KP5, KP6, KP13, KP14, KP15, KP16), and neither a price-checked AS1 nor AS2 is present. A KP15 at threshold (rebutted, so not killing alone) or a confirmed KP18 removes that protection: a paid substitute proves demand, not that serving it pays or that the demand outlives the trend.
- Rule J1: when the pattern sheet gives DEAD and the judge, after reading the sheet, holds another verdict, the verdict is `INSUFFICIENT_DATA` with `disputed: true`; the report names the dispute and `to_collect` names the fact that would settle it.
- `ALIVE` rule A1: no strong pattern meets its threshold unrebutted, D2 does not fire, at least one pull signal (AS1, AS2 or AS3) is present with a confirmed finding that carries a number (a quantity of money, people or units, not a date) or, for AS1 and AS3, is confirmed on two different sites; the pull is corroborated (a second distinct alive signal, or AS1 or AS3 itself confirmed on two different sites); at most one pattern fires (at most two when two or more pull signals are present), KP7 does not fire (unless it names a `clear_market`, section 3), and KP4 does not fire unless an AS1 is price-checked. ALIVE also needs the kill check to have run (`killcheck.json` with checked causes), an explicit entry with an argument for KP2, KP3, KP6 and KP9 (none marked `unknown: true`, and KP9 not left unresolved on assumptions or inside the margin band), and competitors that were measured (`invisible_player_risk` not `high`). An unmeasured ceiling or competitor field blocks ALIVE exactly like a measured weak one, so a thorough run is never worse off than a short one. An obligation (AS2) or unpriced payments show the work must be done, not that buyers will pay this idea's price.
- `INSUFFICIENT_DATA` rule I1: everything else. The report names the missing facts that would move it to DEAD or ALIVE.

The burden of proof sits on both ends. Junk cannot hide in the middle: when three medium signals point to death and nobody pays or is required to pay, the idea dies. Real successes are protected: when a pull signal exists and the kill evidence is mixed, the result is `INSUFFICIENT_DATA`, never `DEAD`.

## 2. Evidence rules for every pattern and signal
- A pattern or signal counts only when at least one of its finding IDs has `opened: true` and `quote_check: found` and the audit did not mark it `fails`. Structural facts about the idea itself (KP9 ceiling arithmetic, KP10 no single-player value) may use `basis: "card"` with the card field named, and still need the numbers or description on the card.
- Counted toward DEAD (D1, D2) are only fired patterns whose decisive claim in `audit.json` (matched by `code`) is `holds`, whose basis is findings rather than the card, whose deciding inputs are findings (`inputs: "findings"` on KP4, KP9, KP15 and KP16; a below-threshold KP9, KP15 or KP16 without it counts as assumed), and whose supporting findings are not labelled `assumption`. A `weak` claim still fires the pattern, so it blocks ALIVE, and it goes to `to_collect`. A `fails` claim fires nothing. `bin/decide` prints each uncounted pattern with its reason in `to_collect`; copy those into the verdict's `to_collect`.
- Independence for D2: each counted pattern needs its own verified finding on its own site. One finding, or two pages of one site, cannot carry two patterns; `bin/decide` pairs patterns with sites and counts only the pairs it can make. KP4 and KP6 together count as one pattern: a price above what the payer pays for a step on which the payer pays nothing is KP6 said twice.
- A quote must not stop before a qualifying clause. A rule quoted without its exception ("units cannot buy software with points" without "for own funds they buy directly from the maker") is a false fact. When a cited quote cuts such a clause, cite a finding with the whole sentence or write `qualifier_read.<finding id>` on the entry with why the clause does not change the argument; `bin/check-run` flags it from the cached page.
- Absence of search results is never evidence for a pattern, with one exception: KP2 and KP6 are shown by what the target people do today (they use a free option at scale; every payer candidate was searched and only free providers were found). The search must be listed in the answer card's `search_matrix`.
- A "what changed" item that is itself a new free substitute (a platform feature, a free official service) counts for KP2 or KP3, never as AS6.
- Evidence published after `as_of`, and the evaluated company itself, never count.
- The idea judged is the frozen card `card.v1.json`. A pivot or a branch is judged beside it and never replaces it.

## 3. Strong kill patterns KP1-KP7, KP9, KP13-KP16
A strong pattern that meets its threshold kills alone (D1). Below its threshold, or rebutted, it still fires as one pattern for D2.

### KP1 Consumer tarpit
- Definition: a consumer idea many people like and many have tried, where earlier attempts died and nothing structural changed. Typical forms: social network for friends, "where to go tonight", music-social, recommendation aggregator, niche dating, habit tracker, event discovery.
- Threshold: (a) two or more dead attempts with the same user, job and revenue model (Q3 `same_idea: yes`, or `partly` when only the brand or city differs), AND (b) no dated change that removes their cause of death, AND (c) no behavioural evidence that users spend money or measurable time on a workaround (Q2 spend empty).
- Fires below threshold: one same-model death, or the category is on the tarpit list above and users pay nothing for the job.

### KP2 Satisfied free or built-in substitute (F1)
- Definition: the target people already do the job with something free (a built-in platform feature, group chats, the state, volunteers, relatives) and are not switching.
- Threshold: a named free substitute covers the idea's core job, the target group uses it (usage numbers, or it ships by default on their phones or tools), and the idea has no axis on which it is several times better that users demonstrably care about (complaints about that exact axis, or spending on a workaround). A quote saying "I am satisfied" is not required; continued use with no paid alternative gaining traction is the evidence.
- Partial only (fires below threshold): the target group is shown to use the free option, but it is rationed, irregular, or misses the key output. A free option that merely exists, with no evidence the target people use it, does not fire.
- Not KP2: the person the product assists doing the job by hand today. When the idea is a tool for that person (an advisor for the drone crew, a checklist for the carer, a copilot for the operator), the crew, carer or operator doing it without the tool is the user and the baseline, not a free substitute. Example: "the escort drone crew already guides the ground robot by watching it from a Mavic" describes the user's current work that the advisor speeds up; it is not KP2. KP2 fires only when a different free option (a built-in feature, a free service, volunteers doing the job instead of the user) covers the idea's output. A substitute that misses the key output is the idea's niche, never KP2 at threshold.

### KP3 The platform or category leader already ships it
- Definition: the product the buyer already pays for, or the platform the idea runs on, ships the same function to the same buyer at no extra charge.
- Threshold: the incumbent's function is live before `as_of` (product page, changelog or help article), is included at no extra charge in a product the payer already pays for or uses (a bundled feature, a free tier, a free manufacturer service), and the idea's claimed difference fits in one changelog sentence. Also at threshold: a paid product the payer already uses (the category leader they subscribe to) already includes the idea's distinguishing feature itself (the thing the pitch says is new, such as a messaging channel or an automation), with no AS4 evidence of complaints about that product on the idea's axis. Otherwise a paid product or paid add-on sold by an incumbent is a competitor, never KP3 at threshold: many successes (payroll, point of sale, design tools) entered markets that large paid vendors already served, and they won on an axis buyers complained about.
- Fires below threshold: the incumbent's no-extra-charge version is weaker or adjacent, or reaches a different buyer. Also below threshold, the closing gap: two or more dated improvements before `as_of` by the platform, the category leader or a general AI tool the target people already use, each on the idea's own axis (speed, limits, a missing feature) and each included at no extra charge in what the payer already pays for or uses, so the gap the idea sells into is shrinking (changelogs, release notes). Paid modules or add-ons sold by the leader are competitors, never the closing gap. A paid competitor, even an exact clone, never fires KP3: it is a positioning question, and AS1 or AS4 evidence when its buyers pay and complain. Search in the users' language before deciding whether a free bundled version exists.

### KP4 Price above what the payer pays (F4)
- Definition: the idea's price per unit is well above what the payer spends on the same step today, above the payer's income for the purpose, or above the harm the product prevents. This is the demand side of the arithmetic; the cost of delivering the product is KP15.
- Threshold: the price is more than 1.5 times the payer's sourced current cost of the same step with no 10x axis (3 times for a consumer impulse purchase: an individual buys it for themselves at USD 50 or less per purchase, where a brand, packaging and presentation premium is normal; set `consumer_impulse: true` in `q6.json`) (the same step means the same deliverable: when the priced substitute lacks the idea's key output, the reason KP2 is only partial, the price route fires below threshold only); OR the price exceeds the payer's sourced income for the purpose (a subscription above a pension); OR the fix costs more than the harm it prevents. A threshold met in any market or segment the idea names is met; missing data for another market does not lower it. The payer's current cost must come from a source dated on or before `as_of` (a dated page, an archive snapshot, a dated price list); an undated live page cannot carry the threshold, whatever the argument about how prices moved since.
- Fires below threshold: the price comparison fails but the idea's price or the payer's current cost is an assumption or comes from an undated source; or the payer works on thin margins shown by a finding (sector margin, founder or trade press quotes) and the price is a noticeable share of that margin.

### KP5 Repeat death of the same model (F5)
- Definition: the same user, job and revenue model already failed two or more times of the same cause.
- Threshold: two or more same-model deaths (Q3 `same_idea: yes`) sharing a cause class, cause quotes found, and the what-changed search (`what_changed.status` `nothing`, or `changed` with no change that removes that cause).
- Fires below threshold: one same-model death, or several `partly` deaths of one cause.

### KP6 No payer (F3)
- Definition: nobody spends money on this job today; only the state or charities help for free, or the people who suffer cannot pay.
- Threshold: Q4 searched every payer candidate (the user, the employer, an insurer, the state budget, a sponsor, donations and volunteer fundraising, charitable funds, the buyer's own budget outside the formal programme such as a unit's own funds, and a manufacturer who bundles the product into its own) and found only free providers or explicit statements that nobody pays; `payer_exists: no`. For a free consumer product with advertising or "venues pay later" revenue, the payer is the advertiser: the threshold is met when advertisers in this category pay only for an audience the product does not have and no comparable product shows advertiser revenue.
- Never at threshold because a law or programme is not yet in force at `as_of`: that is KP11 (trigger not yet there), and the verdict should say when to recheck.
- Before firing, look for an institutional payer under a mandate (municipality, insurer, employer, school); the consumer version of an idea often dies where the institutional version lives.
- A payer that exists but earns from the problem continuing is KP13, not KP6.
- Fires below threshold: a payer exists only in theory (a budget line with no purchase found) after every payer candidate above was searched; Q5 finds no channel within the price band; or the model is free first with "monetise later" and comparable free-first products show large usage but little revenue. A payer who buys an adjacent step today (the same buyers paying for a neighbouring product through the same route) is a payer, not "only in theory". A killcheck cause KP6 `refuted` blocks KP6 unless a new verified finding shows the opposite.
- Never KP6 from a rule that closes one payment channel. A norm that stops one source of money ("programme points cannot buy software") while another route pays (own funds, a fund, volunteers, a maker bundle) is a `nominal` constraint (KP7 section below): record it in `law.json`, name the open routes, and use it as a channel risk; its findings cannot carry KP6.

### KP7 Law forbids it (F2)
- Definition: a named norm in force at `as_of` bans the model, or requires a licence the proposed founders cannot obtain in about 12 months.
- Threshold: `law.json` status `forbidden`, or `licence_needed` with `obtainable: no`, from a statute or regulator page, in force at `as_of`, naming this activity.
- Professional monopoly: a statute in force at `as_of` that reserves the idea's deliverable itself (tax advice, legal advice, a medical act, an audit opinion) to licensed professionals meets the threshold (`licence_needed`, `obtainable: no`) only when a verified finding also shows the reservation applies to this kind of service as sold: a regulator or court decision, an enforcement action or official guidance against unlicensed providers of it, or a change in force aimed at it; and no provider in the cards sells the same service lawfully without the licence. When comparable providers operate by preparing the work for a licensed party who signs it, or the finding shows the reservation only in general terms, KP7 fires below threshold (it still blocks ALIVE).
- A ban or licence in some of the named markets only: KP7 fires below threshold, and when `law.json` shows another named market `allowed` where a pull signal's finding sits, the entry names that place in `clear_market`. A KP7 with `clear_market` does not block ALIVE; the blocked markets go to `to_collect`. KP7 meets its threshold only when every named market is forbidden or needs a licence that cannot be obtained.
- Fires below threshold: a licence or certification is needed and slow; the norm is adopted but not yet in force; or a primary source names a condition the model must meet and a second finding shows the condition is likely unmet for this model (comparable data or secrecy protection required and the destination country has no adequacy decision; a licence that the named founders cannot hold). Unknown, unchecked or merely possible legal requirements never fire KP7: they go to `to_collect` as a legal question.
- Constraint records decide KP7, not the text of a norm. Every norm the run relies on is a record in `law.json.constraints` (schema §8): who it binds (`binds`, `on_whom`), the sanction, enforcement seen or not, whether it closes one payment channel or all of them (`channel_scope`), an unavoidable gate (customs, accreditation, a payment system, a licence register), the open routes with evidence of money, and the share of the payer's money it closes. `bin/decide` computes the class: `unknown` when no verified finding of the norm itself was read (or only headings); `binding` when the sanction is criminal for the seller or founders (export without a permit, state secrets, weapons brokering), or the seller is bound and enforcement is seen, or an unavoidable gate closes all channels with no open route that shows money; `nominal` when the norm closes one channel and a lawful open route shows money (a verified purchase, fundraiser or order through it); `conditional` otherwise. KP7 cites its records in `constraint_ids`. Only `binding` fires KP7 at threshold (and only over all channels); below threshold KP7 needs `binding`, or `conditional` with a verified finding that the condition is likely unmet for this model (`condition_unmet_ids`). `nominal` and `unknown` records never fire KP7 and never carry KP6: they go to Q5 as channel risks, to KP9 as a smaller ceiling (`share_closed`), and to `to_collect`. Mass breach is not an open route for criminal rules: the criminal liability of the seller stays binding.
- Advertising and promotion rules never fire KP7. A norm that limits how the product may be advertised (who may appear in an ad, testimonials, before-and-after images, claims wording, influencer disclosure, medical or financial advertising limits) restricts a marketing tactic, not the model; agencies routinely work around such norms (actors instead of licensed professionals, education and entertainment formats instead of direct offers, disclosed paid partnerships, other channels). Record such a norm as a channel risk in Q5 and in `to_collect`, never as KP7. KP7 covers only norms that ban the product or the business model itself, or require a licence the founders cannot obtain. A fired KP7 without `clear_market` blocks ALIVE: an idea with an open legal condition in all its markets is at best INSUFFICIENT_DATA, with the condition in `to_collect`.

### KP9 Tiny ceiling
- Definition: even the whole first market plus the adjacent markets the idea names cannot pay for a small team.
- Sheet fields: `value_usd` (the quantity compared: the 100% ceiling for the threshold, the 10% share or the 100% venture ceiling below it), `threshold_usd` (the bound it is compared with), `inputs` (`findings` when every count and per-buyer quantity is a finding, the idea's own card price allowed; `assumption` when any count, order size, units per buyer or buyer share is assumed). A KP9 that is not fired still states its computed `value_usd`, or `unknown: true` when the ceiling could not be computed from findings.
- Margin band: a value within 20% of its bound (at or above 0.8 times it) is not a kill signal; KP9 then goes to `to_collect` with the inputs that would settle it. KP9 on assumed inputs never counts toward DEAD. Either way it still blocks ALIVE, because the ceiling is unresolved.
- Threshold: a bottom-up ceiling from sourced counts (buyers or events per year times the idea's price) at 100% share is below USD 1M a year. Count only the buyers who can pay the idea's price (for a six-figure contract, the firms with a budget of that size, not the whole industry's spend); a top-down market size in dollars is never the ceiling. Counts must be findings; a ceiling computed from card assumptions fires below threshold only.
- Fires below threshold: a realistic 10% share of the sourced ceiling is below USD 1M a year; or, for `trajectory: venture`, the 100% ceiling is below USD 10M a year.
- Not KP9: a small market today with a dated driver growing it (that is AS2 or AS6); a market the idea can leave for a named adjacent one with sourced counts (include them in the ceiling).

### KP13 Payer incentive conflict
- Definition: the party that would pay earns from the problem continuing, so the more the product works, the less the payer earns or the smaller its budget. Examples: a weight-loss clinic paid monthly while the patient stays on the drug, asked to buy a programme that takes patients off it; a platform asked to fund a tool that claws back its own fees; a lender asked to pay for a tool that cuts the penalties it earns.
- Threshold: a cited source (opened, quote found) shows how the payer earns from the problem (per-month drug fee, penalty income, fee share, headcount-based budget), and the product's success removes that income or budget, and no mandate in force obliges the payer to buy (no AS2). The argument names the payer, the income line and the finding.
- Fires below threshold: the conflict is plausible from the business model, but no source shows the payer's income line.
- Not KP13: a conflict for a party that does not pay (displaced consultants, the platform when the restaurant pays); a payer that saves money when the product works; a payer that sells the product's outcome at a higher margin than the problem (a clinic that charges more for the maintenance programme than it loses on the drug, shown by a finding).

### KP14 Buyer wants a person or the result, not a tool
- Definition: the payer does not want to do the job with a tool. Two forms: (a) trusted person: the payer pays or relies on a specific person for this job (a tutor, carer, nanny, bookkeeper, lawyer, doctor, trainer, a relative or neighbour) because of trust, accountability, skill or the relationship itself, and the idea replaces that person with software, AI or an interchangeable roster of a company's workers; (b) done for you: the payer wants the outcome delivered and buys it as a service, while the idea sells a tool, API or platform that needs the payer's own work to produce the outcome. People keep the person they know, and buyers who want a result do not buy a tool, even when it is cheaper.
- Threshold: (a) a verified finding quotes the target people preferring, choosing or keeping the person (a parent who hired a live tutor because the child learns better that way, a family that trusts only a known neighbour with the visit), or the dossier's `needed_but_different` applies and `where_pain_actually_is` names the human service itself; or (b) a verified finding quotes the target buyers asking for the result to be done for them, or shows them buying the done-for-you service while self-serve tools for the same job exist and are not adopted; AND no finding shows the target people switching this job to the idea's form (users, retention or growth of a comparable tool or company roster for the same job in the same group).
- Fires below threshold: the dossier (aspects "Whom they trust", "Mindset", "How they cope now") or behaviour suggests trust in a person over a system, or a preference for done-for-you over tools (agencies and freelancers dominate the job; tools in the niche have few users), without direct quotes from the target people.
- Rebuttal: AS3 only, and only when it shows measurable switching to the idea's form for this job (paying users of a comparable app or tool among these people, a company roster with repeat bookings). AS1 never rebuts it: money paid to the person or the service proves they are trusted or wanted, not that a tool can replace them.
- Not KP14: the idea is a tool the trusted person or the service provider uses (a tutor's planning app, a bookkeeper's software, an agency's tool), so the person stays; the idea itself delivers the result as a service (software plus people); the person is used only for a commodity step people already buy from interchangeable providers (a courier, a cleaner booked by the hour on a platform with repeat bookings shown).
- For a platform that sells a human service (care, tutoring, cleaning, repairs, consulting), check KP14 together with KP12: when the client trusts one person, that person is the product and the relationship leaves the platform after the first match.

### KP15 Cost to serve eats the price
- Definition: delivering one unit costs about as much as or more than the price: couriers, warehouses, returns, kitchen labour, licensed data, AI tokens, hosting, support, or acquisition, so each customer loses money and growth deepens the loss.
- Threshold: the realistic case of `economics-template.md` fails (`realistic_case_fails: true`: contribution per customer at or below 0, or realistic CAC above realistic lifetime revenue), the inputs that make it fail are findings, not assumptions, and the cost is part of the model rather than one company's choice: either two or more same-model deaths or post-mortems cite this cost, or the deciding cost inputs come from two or more independent providers or price lists. When the optimistic case also fails (`optimistic_case_fails: true`) the gap cannot be closed and no rebuttal is allowed (`unfixable: true`).
- Fires below threshold: the realistic case fails but a deciding input is an assumption; or one company's post-mortem names this cost while comparable companies with the same model live (the company's cost, not the model's).
- Rebuttal: a sourced cost-down or price path with a number, written as AS5 (the same model runs with public margins elsewhere) or AS6 (a dated drop in the deciding cost); never when `unfixable: true`.

### KP16 Fragmented integration
- Definition: the product is useful only after it connects to a system the customer already runs (a medical record system, point of sale, ERP, bank, accounting package, a state registry), those systems or the rules for them differ between customers or countries, and each connection costs more than the small customers it unlocks pay.
- Threshold: all three are findings: (a) the buyers need the integration to use the product (buyer reviews, competitors' docs or post-mortems saying the product is not used without it); (b) the target customers are split across many systems or rule sets, with no system holding half of them (market-share data, a list of per-country rules or formats); (c) the cost of one integration (vendor fees, certification, engineering quotes, a post-mortem figure) is above ten times the idea's annual price per customer, so each integration needs more than ten customers on that one system just to pay back.
- Fires below threshold: (a) and (b) are findings and the integration cost is an assumption; or the integration route runs through a gatekeeper with an app-store or partner programme whose fees or approval time are unknown.
- Rebuttal: AS5 (the same model sells to the same small customers with public economics), or AS6 (a dated interoperability rule or a middleware provider whose per-customer price is below the idea's price makes connections cheap).
- Not KP16: the product is sold to the party that suffers from the fragmentation (a unified API to many HR, payroll or accounting systems sold to software vendors: that is AS1 for the integrator); the output is useful standalone (users copy it by hand and say so); the target customers are few and large enough to pay for their own integration.

## 4. Supporting kill patterns KP8, KP10-KP12, KP17, KP18
Supporting patterns never kill alone. They count toward D2.

- KP8 Resigned users (refuted by any confirmed AS1: spending on the job is not resignation): people describe the problem but spend nothing, search for nothing and use no workaround (dossier aspect 13 resigned or "nice to have"; Q2 spend empty), or a cheap app in the niche already vanished. Evidence is what people do, not what they say: dropping the course, skipping the step, low installs or reviews of a cheap app, no paid tutors or services advertised, an official statement that the outcome carries no consequence. A complaint alone never fires it; spending on a workaround refutes it. Also fires for a preventive product against a rare event (under about 1% of buyers a year) that nobody budgets for before it happens. Not for a new market: there look for non-consumption instead.
- KP10 Two-sided cold start with no day-one value: neither side gets value until the other side is present and there is no single-player mode; the paying side pays only for an audience.
- KP11 Trigger gone: the event that creates the pain was cancelled or postponed, or the market lives on a subsidy being cut. Cancelled with no other driver: fires. Postponed past the idea's first year (a deadline extended, a scheme prolonged): fires, with the recheck date in `to_collect`. Needs a dated finding of the cancellation, extension or cut.
- KP17 One-off or rare job: the job happens once or rarely per customer (a single exam, a one-time setup, an event every few years), or usage data shows people set it and forget it, while the model needs repeat use (a subscription, per-use fees, assets under management) to recover acquisition cost. Evidence is behaviour: usage frequency, retention or login data, founders' words, the event's own frequency. Refuted by repeat purchase or retention data for a comparable product, or a one-off price that covers acquisition cost.
- KP18 Fad without hold: demand for the category rides a short-video trend and the idea has nothing that keeps the buyer after the trend (no brand, person, format, collection or repeat use), while generic sellers can copy it within weeks (unbranded listings of the same item, dupes); or comparable products spiked and faded within months (sales or search down by half after the peak). Evidence: the fade, the dupes, the trend's decline. Refuted by repeat purchase or brand search persisting a year after a comparable peak.
- KP12 Leakage: in a services marketplace, provider and customer can deal directly after the first match and both gain; no payment protection, insurance or budget routing keeps them.

## 5. Alive signals AS1-AS7
Desk research reaches at most "people already pay". `ALIVE` means clearly viable on public evidence, not validated with customers.

- AS1 Buyers already pay for an inferior substitute (pull): the idea's payer spends money today on the same job (a legacy tool, a consultant, staff, a service) and the substitute is worse on the idea's axis (complaints, cost, time). A number is expected (price, spend, headcount, market spend); without one, AS1 still counts when two different sites confirm that this payer pays for the substitute. List AS1 whenever the evidence shows the payer paying today; never drop it only because the number is missing. Not AS1: paying the incumbent that already ships the idea's function (that is KP3); spend on a different step of the problem than the one the idea does; spend the idea cannot replace (a fee the law requires to go to a licensed party, a done-for-you service when the payer would still need it after buying the idea); money paid to a trusted person the idea would replace (a live tutor, a carer, a bookkeeper, a relative) when KP14 fires or the dossier's `needed_but_different` puts the pain in that paid service; and any spend when the idea's price per unit is above what the payer pays today for the same step (KP4 price route). In that last case mark the KP4 entry `price_route: true` and `bin/decide` ignores AS1. Set `price_checked: true` on AS1 when the idea's price was compared with the payer's current cost per unit of the same step and the ratio (`q6.price_vs_current_cost`) is at or below 1.5; benchmark or assumed inputs on the idea's side are acceptable when the current-cost side rests on a finding. `bin/check-run` enforces the q6 ratio in both directions (above 1.5: KP4 fired with `price_route: true`; at or below: AS1 `price_checked: true`) unless `verdict.json` has `price_override` with a cited reason why the q6 ratio compares a different step. Only a price-checked AS1 rebuts a kill pattern, blocks D2 or makes an idea ALIVE: unchecked spend on staff or tools proves the job is paid for, not that this price fits. An unchecked AS1 stays on the sheet as context. Every AS1 names its `substitute`: `tool` (software, a device, a legacy system), `service` (a company or agency whose workers are interchangeable to the payer) or `human` (a specific person the payer chose: tutor, carer, bookkeeper, lawyer, doctor, relative). `bin/decide` ignores an AS1 marked `human` whenever KP14 fires with a confirmed finding, and any AS1 marked `pain_in_substitute: true`, which the conductor sets when the dossier's `needed_but_different` applies and `where_pain_actually_is` names the service this AS1 pays for (live instruction, personal care); the payer then pays for the very thing the idea does not deliver. `bin/check-run` rejects an AS1 without `substitute`, and, when the dossier's `needed_but_different` applies, an AS1 without `pain_in_substitute` set to true or false.
- AS2 Mandate in force (pull): a dated rule already in force at `as_of` creates recurring obligatory work that the idea does or cheapens. Adopted but not in force: not AS2; note it for `LATER`.
- AS3 Measurable pull: prepayments, letters of intent, waitlists with numbers, repeat use, fast public growth of a close substitute, many buyers asking for exactly this. Only people-sourced data.
- AS4 Crowded but disliked: many competitors with low ratings or complaints on the idea's axis, and no liked leader above half the market.
- AS5 The same model works elsewhere with public economics (revenue, customers, margin) and the transfer conditions hold here (law, payer, language).
- AS6 A dated change removes the cause that killed earlier attempts (technology, law, cost, behaviour, distribution).
- AS7 Proven short-video channel: comparable ordinary products in the same category and buyer group grew mainly through short video (TikTok, Reels, Shorts), shown by two or more cases with numbers (sell-outs, sales jumps, TikTok Shop GMV, downloads) before `as_of`, and the idea can show its value on camera within seconds (a visual proof moment: before and after, a reveal, a texture, a transformation, a ritual or recipe others can copy) or through a person (a founder or staff on camera). AS7 is not a pull signal and never makes an idea ALIVE alone; it corroborates a pull, and it rebuts KP1 and KP5 when the earlier attempts died of distribution or acquisition cost rather than of the product. Evidence: the cases, plus category signals (hashtag views, TikTok Creative Center top products, category GMV).

Reverse signal, recorded as KP8: a cheap foreign incumbent that has existed for years and never scaled.

## 6. Rebuttals: which signal can cancel which pattern
A rebutted strong pattern no longer kills alone (no D1) but still fires for D2. List the rebutting codes in the pattern's `rebutted_by`.

| Pattern | Can be rebutted by |
|---|---|
| KP1 tarpit | AS3, AS6, AS7 |
| KP2 free substitute | AS1, AS3 |
| KP3 incumbent ships it | AS4 aimed at that incumbent's version |
| KP4 price above what the payer pays | a sourced cost-down or price path with a number, written as AS5 or AS6 |
| KP15 cost to serve | a sourced cost-down or price path with a number, written as AS5 or AS6; never when `unfixable: true` |
| KP16 fragmented integration | AS5, AS6 |
| KP9 tiny ceiling | nothing (recompute with the named adjacent markets) |
| KP5 repeat death | AS6 aimed at the shared cause; AS7 when the shared cause was distribution or acquisition cost |
| KP6 no payer | AS1, AS2 |
| KP8 resigned users | any confirmed AS1 removes it (spending on a workaround) |
| KP7 law | nothing |
| KP13 payer incentive conflict | AS2 (a mandate in force obliges the payer to buy anyway) |
| KP14 buyer wants a person or the result | AS3 showing measurable switching to the idea's form for this job |

AS1 rebuts only when `price_checked` is true.

## 7. False kills and the Airbnb test
Never a pattern on their own: competitors exist; the market is crowded; demand is not visible online; no competitors were found; the team lacks a skill; the market looks small now (except KP9, section 3); the idea looks like a toy or boring; an incumbent could copy it someday.

Airbnb test: before keeping a supporting pattern, ask whether the same argument would have killed two of Airbnb 2008, Dropbox 2007 and Stripe 2010. If yes, drop that supporting pattern (`airbnb: "dropped"`). The test never cancels a strong pattern that meets its threshold, and never clears D2 on its own; only a rebuttal from section 6 does that.

Protected successes, and what separated them from junk: Granola, Dropbox and Grammarly beat a free option on an axis people visibly complained about (AS1/AS4). Airbnb had hotels as a paid inferior substitute (AS1). Duolingo had paid courses as a substitute and fast measurable retention (AS1, AS3). Mable and OpenEvidence had a payer other than the user (government budgets, pharma). When such a signal is present, the kill evidence is mixed and the verdict is at worst `INSUFFICIENT_DATA`.

## 8. Pattern sheet: how to fill it
The conductor writes `patterns` and `alive_signals` into `verdict.json`:
- One `patterns` entry for every code KP1-KP18, fired or not: `{"code", "strength": "strong|supporting", "fired": true|false, "threshold_met": true|false, "finding_ids": [], "basis": "findings|card", "argument": "one or two sentences citing answer cards", "rebutted_by": [], "airbnb": "kept|dropped|n/a"}`. `threshold_met` is always false for supporting patterns. KP4 adds `price_route` (true when the price per unit is above the payer's sourced current cost of the same step). KP15 adds `unfixable`. KP7 adds `clear_market` (a place from `law.json` with status `allowed`, or null) and `constraint_ids` (the `law.json.constraints` records it rests on). KP4, KP9, KP15 and KP16 add `inputs` (`findings` or `assumption`) when fired. KP9 adds `value_usd` and `threshold_usd`, or `unknown: true`. KP2, KP3, KP6 and KP9 must each carry an argument; set `unknown: true` when the run could not assess one (it blocks ALIVE). An entry may add `qualifier_read: {"<finding id>": "why the clause after the quote does not change the argument"}`.
- One `alive_signals` entry per signal present: `{"code": "AS1".."AS7", "finding_ids": [], "number": "the number the signal carries, or null", "price_checked": true|false (AS1 only), "substitute": "tool|service|human" (AS1 only), "pain_in_substitute": true|false (AS1 only; required when the dossier's `needed_but_different` applies), "argument": "str"}`.
- Then run `python3 <PLUGIN_ROOT>/bin/decide <RUN>/verdict.json`. It prints the verdict and rule. Use them; if you disagree, fix the sheet with a better citation, never the verdict.
